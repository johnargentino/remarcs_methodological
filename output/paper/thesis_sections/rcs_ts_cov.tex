\section{Modeling RCS Data with a Univariate Time Series}


Given such a dataset, it may seem apt to simply calculate averages at each time step and analyze these averages as a time series with covariates. Such a model is an example of a state-space model that can be modeled jointly via Kalman filtering \cite{BrockwellDavis1991}. In the regression setting, aggregates would be needed for both response and explanatory variables, fitting the following model:

$$ \bar{\mathbf{y}} = (n^{-1}X_t'X_f)\beta + \mathbf{u}, \qquad \mathbf{u}\sim\text{ARMA(p,q)} $$

  This method is very effective at teasing out population-level fixed effects like intercepts and slopes, but is limited when it comes to the estimation of individual-level effects. A scenario in which this method falls short is the presence of indicator variables that vary little in their distribution at each time step. This is a highly plausible scenario when it comes to sampling methods for surveys. If researchers are interested in the effect of a certain binary covariate, for example, they may very well aim to be consistent in the proportions of time step samples that have this feature. Such consistency hinders the ability of the significant effect to be estimated as such, which we illustrate through a simulated simple regression model with a trend and autocorrelated errors:

  $$ y_{ti} = \beta_0 + \beta_1t + \beta_2x_{ti} + u_t + \varepsilon_{ti}, \qquad i = 1,...,n_t$$

  $$ u_t - \phi u_{t-1} = \eta_t + \theta \eta_{t-1}, \qquad t = 1,...,T$$ 
  
  
  $$\eta_t \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}N(0,\sigma_\eta^2), \qquad x_{ti} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(.5), \qquad \varepsilon_{ti} \overset{\text{i.i.d}}{\sim} N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$$
