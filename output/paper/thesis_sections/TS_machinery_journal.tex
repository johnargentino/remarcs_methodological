\section{Time Series Fundamentals}




We begin by introducing some machinery and notation from time series analysis before detailing existing methods. Unless otherwise stated, all results in this chapter are from Brockwell and Davis (1991) \citep{BrockwellDavis1991}. If $\{X_t\}_{t=1}^T$ is a time series such that Var$(X_t) < \infty$ $\forall$ $t$, then the autocovariance function (ACVF) $\gamma_X(\cdot,\cdot)$ is defined by $\gamma_X(r,s) = \text{Cov}(X_r,X_s), \qquad r,s \in \{t\}$ 






For \emph{weakly stationary} time series, the ACVF can be more simply parameterized as $\gamma_X(h):= \text{Cov}(X_t,X_{t+h})$. The autocorrelation function (ACF) $\rho_X(h)$ of a stationary time series is then defined as $\rho_X(h) = \gamma_X(h)/\gamma_X(0)$.


A time series is \emph{strictly stationary} if:


\begin{itemize}
    \item[] $(X_{t_1},...X_{t_k})' \overset{d}{=} (X_{t_1 + h},...X_{t_k + h})$ for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$ and $t_1,...,t_k,h \in \mathbb{Z}$
\end{itemize}

For the purposes of this paper, ``stationary'' will refer to the weak variety. 
 

A time series is said to be white noise with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2$ if and only if:

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:wn}
    \gamma_X(h) = \begin{cases}
        \sigma^2, & h = 0 \\
        0, & h \neq 0
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
