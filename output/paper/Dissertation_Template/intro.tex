\section{Formatting Mathematics}

Here are some examples of mathematical statements. In the mathematical sciences, equations should be formatted clearly and numbered where appropriate. 

\begin{theorem}
Let $X$ be a topological space. Then the identity map $id: X \to X$ is continuous.
\end{theorem}

Display equations look like this:
\begin{equation}
    e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0
\end{equation}

And you can also do sequences of equations:
\begin{align}
    f(x) &= x^2 + 2x + 1 \\
         &= (x + 1)^2
\end{align}

If you need to define a term, you can use a glossary entry like \gls{math}. You can also index important terms like \index{topology}topology.

If you need to include a long quotation (four or more lines), you should format it as a block quote. The guidelines state:
\begin{quote}
Short quotations of prose appear in the text within quotation marks. Quotations that are four or more lines long in the typed text should be set off from the text in single-spacing and indented in its entirety four spaces from the left margin, with no quotation marks, and separated from the text above and below by a double-space. Paragraph indention of quoted material should be indicated by a four-space indention within the block quotation.
\end{quote}
The {\tt tulane-math-dissertation} package automatically configures the {\tt quote} and {\tt quotation} environments to obey these single-spacing rules while preserving the double-spacing everywhere else.
